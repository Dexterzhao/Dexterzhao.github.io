Graphics research notes

Study topics and planned experiments

Rendering and 3D assets

My broader interests are real-time rendering and generated 3D content for game engines. The following topics guide my paper reading and planned implementation work.

Rendering systems
Physically based shading, image-based lighting, shadowing, global illumination, and temporal reconstruction. Questions include how changes in visibility, sampling, or scene detail affect GPU time and temporal artifacts.
GPU and engine architecture
HLSL and compute shaders, GPU-driven culling, level of detail, render graphs, memory bandwidth, and synchronization. UE5 topics of interest include Nanite, Lumen, Virtual Shadow Maps, and temporal super resolution; My focus is on understanding their design.
Neural representations
NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting, meshes, and signed distance fields. I am interested in comparing memory and rendering cost, local editing, relighting, and conversion to representations that an engine can use.
Generated game assets
Geometry and material generation together with topology, UVs, normals, LODs, and collision. A planned comparison is to evaluate generated and manually prepared assets under the same scene, camera, and rendering settings, measuring asset defects, visual quality, and frame time separately.

Terrain generation: current study

I am studying the terrain-generation paper and public implementation below. Baseline replication and evaluation are planned; experimental results are not yet available.

The reference is Alexander Goslin's InfiniteDiffusion: Bridging Learned Fidelity and Procedural Utility for Open-World Terrain Generation, SIGGRAPH 2026 (ACM DOI: 10.1145/3799902.3811080). Goslin's work studies lazy, seed-consistent, unbounded diffusion sampling and applies it to hierarchical terrain generation. Those algorithms and demonstrations belong to the original author.

Terrain research questions

  1. Control versus realism. Can terrain satisfy explicit ridge, river, coastline, and traversability constraints while retaining diverse, plausible landforms?
  2. Local edits versus global consistency. Can artists change a region without disturbing distant terrain, breaking hydrology, or making the result depend on tile request order?
  3. Generation versus interaction. Which representations and scheduling choices preserve visual continuity while meeting a measured frame-time and memory budget?

Planned terrain experiments

01

Baseline replication

Question: What behavior and performance can I reproduce from the released implementation on available hardware?

Experiment: Pin the source commit, dependency versions, model checkpoints, and licenses. Start with inference using released weights; full training is a separate, compute-dependent task. Trace the coordinate/seed interface and hierarchy, then generate identical regions under raster, reverse, and shuffled request orders. Compare with a fixed-canvas diffusion baseline where feasible and a procedural fractal-noise baseline at matched output extent and resolution.

Measure: Height and normal discontinuities at tile edges; repeated-query numerical differences; latency (cold/warm, median/p95); samples or area per second; peak GPU/CPU memory. Report hardware, tile size, resolution, sampling settings, and cache policy alongside every result.

Deliverable: A versioned replication notebook, sample maps, and a discrepancy log. Only mark replication complete after rerunnable outputs and measurements exist.

02

A terrain benchmark with explicit scope

Question: Do improvements generalize beyond a small set of visually appealing samples?

Experiment: First follow the upstream data protocol. Then define a documented, license-compatible digital elevation model (DEM) benchmark covering mountains, plains, coasts, and river basins. Candidate sources include NOAA ETOPO and the upstream terrain/climate data sources, subject to checking coverage, resolution, and use terms. Separate training, validation, and test regions geographically with buffer zones; prevent overlapping crops and resampled duplicates from leaking across splits. Treat any overlap with pretrained-model data as a limitation.

Measure: Compare distributions of elevation, slope, curvature, roughness, and drainage density. Use height RMSE only for paired reconstruction or specified target heights, not arbitrary unpaired generations. Evaluate diversity across fixed seeds, constraint errors, and blinded plausibility judgments when practical; do not rely on a single image-quality score.

Deliverable: A manifest with source versions, coordinate systems, units, pixel spacing, no-data handling, crop rules, splits, seeds, and evaluation scripts.

03

Controllable structure, hydrology & landforms

Question: Which controls offer useful artistic direction without sacrificing physical plausibility?

Experiment: Begin with the reference implementation's existing coarse conditioning. Compare it with proposed ridge/valley sketches, coastline masks, river paths, elevation anchors, and slope or traversability constraints. Test inference-time guidance or post-processing before considering retraining. Sweep constraint strengths and ablate each control separately. Compare hydrology-aware penalties with erosion-based post-processing under a matched time budget.

Measure: Anchor-height error, ridge/river path distance, coastline overlap, slope violations, river connectivity, unintended sinks, and uphill-flow violations. Fix the flow-routing algorithm and thresholds. Plot constraint satisfaction against landform statistics, diversity, and runtime; inspect failure cases such as river crossings and incompatible controls.

Deliverable: A small control suite with explicit success and failure examples. Establish numerical acceptance thresholds on validation regions before testing.

04

Multi-scale & large-world generation

Question: Can a world retain regional structure and local detail under arbitrary navigation and changing resolution?

Experiment: Evaluate the upstream hierarchy first. Then vary coarse context, tile overlap, cache size, and level-of-detail transitions. Query neighboring tiles, distant teleports, and repeated locations; test raster and shuffled orders over expanding world extents. Compare terrain at matched world coordinates across resolutions, including long ridgelines and basin boundaries.

Measure: Boundary height/normal errors, cross-scale height disagreement, drainage connectivity across tiles, visual popping, request-order differences, memory versus explored area, and cold versus cached latency. Distinguish a bounded cache from persistent world state, and report the resolution and query size for scaling claims.

Deliverable: A navigation stress test and a tradeoff report for world coherence, detail, latency, and memory.

05

Materials, vegetation & procedural assets

Question: Can a generated environment remain consistent with terrain and usable as game content?

Experiment: Start with interpretable slope/elevation/moisture rules for material blending and vegetation placement. Compare with a proposed learned placement model only when data and compute permit. Introduce a small licensed asset set before exploring 3D game asset generation. Define integration requirements for scale, topology, UVs, collision, placement, and LODs; compare procedural asset variants with generated variants under equal scene budgets.

Measure: Floating/intersecting objects, placement constraint violations, coverage and diversity, material seams, collision failures, triangle counts, texture memory, draw calls, and GPU time. Use paired scenes to distinguish placement quality from asset appearance.

Deliverable: An asset manifest and reproducible terrain-to-scene pipeline. Asset generation is a later extension, conditional on a stable terrain baseline.

06

Real-time UE5 / Unity integration

Question: What portion of generation and scene updates can fit an interactive rendering and streaming budget?

Experiment: Choose one engine first, then evaluate portability to the other. Build heightfield import and a chunked runtime representation; compare precomputed streaming with asynchronous generation. Profile worker jobs, CPU-to-GPU transfers, mesh/collision creation, material evaluation, vegetation instancing, and LOD switching separately. Test a repeatable camera route, rapid traversal, and teleports with fixed rendering settings.

Measure: CPU/GPU frame time, p50/p95/p99 frame time, streaming stalls, generation latency, peak VRAM/RAM, draw calls, and visible seams/popping. For a proposed 60 fps target, report frames over 16.7 ms and reserve an explicit generation budget; a fast average alone is insufficient.

Deliverable: A small engine demo with captured traces and a performance breakdown. UE5 and Unity are planned integration targets, not completed implementations.

07

Editability & reproducible evaluation

Question: Can a user revise local terrain, save it, and reproduce the same world later?

Experiment: Prototype masks for fixed versus regenerable regions, brush/river edits, and an edit log keyed by seed and world coordinates. Compare local regeneration with full regeneration; test save/reload, undo, and query-order changes. Check whether a local edit requires a wider hydrological repair region, and make that dependency visible.

Measure: Edit-to-preview latency, error inside the edited region, unintended changes outside it, seam/drainage failures, and reproducibility after reload. Separate bitwise determinism on a pinned environment from tolerance-based numerical reproducibility across hardware.

Deliverable: Scripts, configurations, environment lockfiles, seed lists, raw measurements, and a limitations report. Use at least 20 fixed test seeds per condition as an initial protocol; repeat timing runs, include warm-up, and report spread or bootstrap confidence intervals.
